Edge-model architecture × quantization · a hybrid convergence

RANGER

Rotation-Aligned · Nested-precision · Gaussianized · Elastic Representation

Quantizability is a trainable architectural property, not a post-hoc treatment. Every winning quantizer and every winning edge trick fights the same enemy — the outlier structure of weights & activations — from two ends of one causal chain. Co-design the model so its numbers are born incoherent, and low bits stop hurting.

§1

The one equation everything orbits

A uniform quantizer has exactly two dials. The entire field — rotations, codebooks, ternary, microscaling, QAT — is a different way to move one of them.

STEP & ERROR · b BITS OVER RANGE R = max|x|
Δ = R / 2b
MSE ≈ Δ²/12 = R² / (12·22b)

Error falls 4× per bit — but rises with the square of the largest value. One outlier that doubles R costs a full bit of precision for every weight sharing that scale.

R ↓ Suppress outliers: AWQ (scale) · QuaRot/SpinQuant (rotate) · VQ/trellis (change the geometry) · Super-Weight/OWQ (exempt them) · QK-Norm / gated attention (kill at source)
spend b Put bits where curvature demands: GPTQ/OBQ · KronQ (Kronecker Hessian) · nested MatFormer precision · per-role formats {2,4,8}
§2

The causal chain — one enemy, seven links

Massive activations, attention sinks, residual sinks and super-weights are the same phenomenon. Quantizers intervene late; architecture can intervene early.

1
pre-norm trainingStability needs a pressure valve. Pre-norm + RMSNorm concentrates gradients on a few tokens. (Attention Sinks Induce Gradient Sinks, 2603.17771)
2
gradient sinkThe model parks a constant "do-nothing" bias on sink tokens (often BOS). (Active-Dormant Heads, 2410.13835)
3
attention / residual sinkSinks are outlier-driven rescaling interacting with RMSNorm. (Unified View of Sinks → W4A4, 2601.22966)
4
massive activationsA few residual dims become 10³–10⁴× larger — learned constant biases. (Massive Activations, 2402.17762 · emerge at one layer, 2605.08504)
5
dynamic range blows upR = max|x| explodes. This is the failure point — the R in §1.
6
error ∝ R²Uniform-quant error explodes; every other weight loses precision.
7
low-bit failsSub-4-bit collapses — unless you intervened upstream.
Why "just clip the outliers" fails: the sink is an adaptive response to a real training need (link 1). Delete it and training destabilizes. You must give the model a cleaner mechanism for the same job — which is why architectural fixes (QK-Norm, gated attention, learnable residual gain) succeed where clipping doesn't.

§4

Four under-exploited couplings

Where an architecture atom and a quantization atom reinforce each other — the "safe crossover points" the theory is built on.

p-RoPE ⊕ rotation

RoPE seeds KV outliers; p-RoPE leaves a (1−p) position-free subspace. That subspace is exactly what QuaRot/SpinQuant can Hadamard-rotate for free — partial RoPE enlarges the rotatable space.

AltUp width ⊕ low bits

Widening by K spreads energy over K× more coords → per-dim variance ↓1/K → more Gaussian, more incoherent → afford ≈½·log₂K fewer bits at equal error. Width and bits are one frontier.

MatFormer ⊕ mixed precision

Nested submodels induce a trained importance ordering — the inner slice must stand alone. That ordering is the bit-allocation prior mixed-precision normally estimates with a Hessian.

QK-Norm / gated attn ⊕ rotation

Kill the sink at the source (Quantizable Transformers → INT8 free) so the residual rotation is small enough to fold into weights — erasing the online-Hadamard latency.

§6

The four pillars — each a falsifiable prediction

1

Incoherence-Native residual

QK-Norm + gated/clipped attention + learnable residual gain so sinks never grow. p-RoPE frees a subspace; a fixed Hadamard folded into weights (zero runtime cost); kurtosis/spectral-decay reg during QAT.

Predict P1W4A4 & W2A8 at <2% drop with no online rotation, vs >8% (or a mandatory online Hadamard) for vanilla. Falsified if it still needs online rotation, or reg costs >1% FP quality.
2

Nested elastic precision

Train a MatFormer family; inner slice at 8-bit/FP, progressively lower-precision shells. Importance ↔ bits aligned by construction. Low-rank FP16 residual (HGF / Mixture-of-LoRAs) catches super-weights.

Predict P2At equal average bits, nested ≥ uniform and ≥ Hessian-sensitivity mixed-precision. Falsified if estimated-sensitivity matches it within noise.
3

Curvature-matched formats

By role, using a cheap Kronecker Hessian (KronQ): FFN→VQ/trellis/GSQ · global KV→rotation+FP4 · local KV→hard low-bit · PLE→CPU codebook · router/super-weights→FP16 · drafter→ternary.

Predict P3Per-role formats beat one global format at equal bytes by ≥1 MMLU pt / ≥5% PPL — Gemma 4's audio {2,4,8} result, generalized to the LLM.
4

Ride the capacity–precision duality

Widen cheaply with AltUp, then spend the incoherence dividend on fewer bits. Co-tune (K, b) toward the equal-marginal-returns point.

Predict P4AltUp-K× widening buys ≈½·log₂K fewer bits at equal quality, and activation kurtosis drops with K. Falsified if widening doesn't lower kurtosis or buy bits. The cleanest single test of the thesis.
§7

The crossover point, formally

The "safe crossover" is where three knobs — bits, AltUp width, nesting depth — buy the same quality per joule. Most systems max one and leave the others on the table.

CO-OPTIMIZATION · EQUAL-MARGINAL-RETURNS (KKT) AT THE OPTIMUM
minimize L(width w, bits b, nesting s) subject to Compute(w,s) + Memory(w,b,s) ≤ Budget ∂L/∂w · (∂Budget/∂w)⁻¹ = ∂L/∂b · (∂Budget/∂b)⁻¹ = ∂L/∂s · (∂Budget/∂s)⁻¹

At the legendary point, one more bit, one more slice of AltUp width, and one more nesting shell all return the same quality per joule.

§8

Bottlenecks, in plain language

The six real obstacles — stated simply, with the first workaround to try. These are the spots where the theory could break, and where a second pair of eyes helps.

A
The spin and the compass want the same knob
To hide loud outliers we "spin" the numbers (rotation). But the model's sense of word position (RoPE) is also a spin on the same numbers — spin everything and you scramble order.
fixRotate only p-RoPE's un-rotated ¾ subspace. Open: learn which dims are positional vs cleanable?
B
1.58-bit is a diet you must be born on
You can't shrink a normal model to ternary after the fact — it must be trained ternary from scratch, which is costly.
fixTernary only the outer shell + drafter; inner core higher-bit; thin FP16 correction lane (HGF). Open: how thin can the lane be?
C
The phone-book problem (VQ lookup)
The best compression looks each weight up in a shared codebook; on a phone NPU, millions of random look-ups are slow even though the file is tiny.
fixTrellis (QTIP) computes instead of looks up; or tuned scalar (GSQ) that nearly matches VQ on any hardware. Open: which does your target chip like?
D
Experts are fragile (MoE)
Each expert only saw a slice of the data, so it's delicate — round it too hard and it breaks.
fixExtra bits on the router + shared expert. Open: do we even want MoE for the edge target, or dense+PLE?
E
Measuring "delicate" costs as much as fixing it
Knowing which weights are sensitive needs the Hessian (curvature), which is expensive.
fixKronQ's Kronecker trick makes it cheap; MatFormer may hand us the ordering free. Open: is trained ordering a good Hessian proxy? (Phase 3 tests it.)
F
"Free" rotation isn't obviously free
Folding the cleanup rotation into weights is free for rotations next to a matmul — not automatically for ones inside attention, and PLE injects a vector mid-stream that may break the fold.
fixSource-suppress so in-attention rotation is unnecessary; fold only linear-adjacent ones. Open: does PLE force a small online step? Check early.
